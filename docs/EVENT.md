## How winning works

- **Challenge prizes:** each challenge partner picks its own winner using its own criteria. Ask the mentor for those criteria early.
- **Overall winner:** face-to-face peer review picks the top 5. They pitch live to a seven-person grand jury:
  Kia Kanninen (Founders House), Daksh Raghuvanshi (AfterQuery), Otto Söderlund (Supercell),
  Timo Ahopelto (Lifeline Ventures), Umar Noor (Google DeepMind), Hubertus van der Drift (Context.dev),
  and Arturs Polis (Verda). Verda says he looks for "prototypes with a clear path to production and scale".
- What the grand jury says it looks for (from the official site):
  - Kia Kanninen (CEO, Founders House): "teams that start from a problem worth solving, not just a feature that's easy to build"
  - Daksh Raghuvanshi (RL Lead, AfterQuery; lead author of StoreBench, a live-commerce agent benchmark where no frontier model
    beat a simple scripted operator): "teams that can explain exactly why an agent fails in their world, and whose reward holds
    when someone tries to break it"
  - Otto Söderlund (AI Lead, Supercell; founded Speechly): "teams that start from a real user problem and ship something people
    want to come back to"
  - Hubertus "Beer" van der Drift (CTO, Context.dev): "teams that move fast, stay pragmatic, and find that one unique insight
    that solves a problem 10x faster or better"
  - Arturs Polis (CTO, Verda): "teams willing to tackle the hardest challenges and build prototypes with a clear path to
    production and scale"
  - Timo Ahopelto (Managing Partner, Lifeline Ventures) and Umar Noor (Developer Experience, Google DeepMind): no criteria published
- Prizes total €100,000; the breakdown is TBD. Teams have 1–4 people. Submissions are open-sourced under the MIT licence.

## Candidate: Stoa "Make Blueprints Talk" (captain's first choice, not final)

- Stoa's investor is Lifeline Ventures, whose managing partner (Timo Ahopelto) sits on the grand jury. The challenge
  is in Verda's track, and Verda's CTO (Arturs Polis) is also on the jury.
- What Stoa asks for: a correct answer **plus where in the drawing it came from**, so a human can verify it in seconds.
  They want approaches next year's frontier models won't make obsolete: hybrids of frontier models, computer vision,
  3D, geometry, or small fine-tuned models.
- Dataset: ~14,000 drawings (PDF, DWG, DXF, IFC, SVG, PNG/JPEG scans) in ~30 languages, ~120,000 questions with
  answers (`questions.jsonl`: path, question, answer, unit, type). Scoring: counts exact; areas, lengths and volumes
  within 2%; text answers free-form.
  - Preview (0.5 GB): https://fwjydgxpttajiqpfmqtj.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/stoa-junction-2026/junction-dataset-preview.zip
  - Full (10 GB): https://fwjydgxpttajiqpfmqtj.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/stoa-junction-2026/junction-dataset.zip
- The preview's 7,990 questions: 76% counts, 20% areas, 3% text. By source:
  - `cubicasa5k`: Finnish floor plans, PNG only. 4,270 count questions (windows, WCs, sinks, saunas, balconies).
  - `swiss_dwellings`: the same drawing as PDF, DXF and PNG. The DXF has clean layers (DOOR, WINDOW, FIXTURE,
    ROOM_OUTLINE, ROOM_TEXT). Counts and room areas.
  - `ifc_models` and `ifc_bench`: IFC models; `ifc_models` PDFs are floor plans rendered from the IFC, and the same
    question is asked on both. Room areas, slab and wall volumes, element counts, open questions.
  - `opening_design`: real US drawing sets; schedule tables with extractable PDF text.
  - `habs`, `finna`, `europeana`, `wikimedia`: scanned drawings with no questions in the preview.
- **Licences:** every file keeps its own licence (`data/<source>/LICENSE.md`): some are NonCommercial or ShareAlike.
  **Never commit the dataset to this repo.** Keep credits when showing a drawing in the demo.
